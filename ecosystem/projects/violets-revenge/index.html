Violet’s Revenge — From Playable Prototype to Funded Development Pilot

A gothic-forensics game and a documented opportunity to test the viability of independent, accessible game production.

Evidence can open an exit—or expose what a player would rather destroy. Violet’s Revenge is an original investigative horror project centered on evidence, agency and the consequences of wrongdoing. The immediate proposition is one coherent, testable Morgue experience.

Current position: browser prototypes and documented development work. This is a development proposition, not a completed game or an approved funding round. Full 1v4 online multiplayer remains a future direction.

1 · Clarify the project

One investigation. Readable consequences.

Gothic horror, forensic investigation and supernatural pursuit meet in a contained Morgue and office. Violet is a methodical, restrained, grief-driven forensics examiner turned supernatural executioner. The Guilty remain masked archetypes, without named identities or redemption backstories.

The documented phase-one loop gives one Guilty player a concrete task: find case files, take them to an incinerator and unlock the exit while Violet hunts. Nightingale sound traps turn movement into evidence; grave-dirt slowing and capture at an autopsy table are documented design targets, not a blanket claim about current implementation.

The initial experience asks whether searching, concealment, pursuit and escape can produce clear decisions in one small environment. A later 1v4 online game, progression, additional maps and transmedia work are long-term directions outside this pilot.

Canon sources: Story bible · Character bible · Phase-one roadmap · Sensitive-content guide.

Expanded treatment and the return from purgatory

The existing public treatment describes Violet alive after the attack; the older franchise roadmap describes death and return. Dominique supplied the connecting sequence for this proposal: in a dream sequence, Violet is in purgatory. A candle nearly burned to its wick restores itself. She feels drawn to take it and finds herself alive; her memories of what happened are repressed.

This is owner-supplied continuity direction, recorded on 7 November 2026, rather than an AI-invented resolution. The current story bible fixes the Examiner → off-screen Attack → Executioner structure, and the character bible controls character claims. The purgatory sequence has not been added to those repository bibles by this editorial change; document its approved placement through the existing canon process before implementing it.

Lady Violet and Sebastian provide historical development lineage. Their conflict concerns unequal responsibility, agency and collective resistance. Dates, source inventory and spelling remain unresolved in the lineage record; ancestral material enters the game only through an approved canon change.

The smallest worthwhile funded milestone

Proposed discovery milestone: independent technical and production review. Review both browser lanes, reproduce rule-test evidence, observe the forensic loop and resolve the scope decision with Dominique. Deliver a risk register, agreed acceptance criteria, staffing assumptions and a priced pilot brief. A worthwhile result can be a narrower scope or a decision to stop.

Proposed pilot: one Morgue experience with a coherent evidence-to-exit loop and readable pursuit/capture outcomes. Preserve the phase-one 1v1 concept as the design baseline. Discovery must decide whether the current hot-seat implementation is the viable pilot lane; it must not quietly substitute a completed local or online multiplayer claim.

  1. Players can identify their role, objective, legal actions and current game state without facilitator correction; record observed confusion.
  2. Searching for case files, processing or destroying evidence, and reaching escape or capture produce readable, repeatable outcomes. Approve the exact rules before implementation.
  3. Input, text size, contrast and non-audio equivalents receive keyboard, touch and assistive-technology review. Record barriers rather than declaring universal accessibility.
  4. A recorded build passes repeatable rules checks and QA reproduction steps; severe progression blockers are fixed or explicitly stop acceptance.
  5. External adult playtest observations, consent-safe feedback and unresolved risks accompany a go, revise or stop recommendation.

Discovery should agree participant numbers, test tasks, severity thresholds and who accepts each deliverable before work is commissioned. Passing tests alone does not authorize the next investment.

No production engine, new backend, dedicated servers or dependency is selected here. Current-stack decision process governs that decision. Full 1v4 networking, console ports, extra maps, franchise production and marketing launch are excluded.

Tentative sequence: discovery at 40–80 paid hours, then a separately commissioned 500-hour pilot. 4 · Prepare the pitch

A useful review can be small

A senior professional can examine an existing loop, a deterministic rules boundary or a specific accessibility barrier without agreeing to build the whole game. Useful review questions include whether a player can explain the evidence-to-exit loop, whether state transitions are reproducible, and which obstacle most threatens a small pilot.

Where agreed, scoped issues and reviewed pull requests provide attributable public evidence of the work. Agree files, time limit, acceptance criteria, reviewer, publication choices, rights and credit before starting. Declining or ending involvement is unrestricted.

Current exploratory participation is voluntary and unpaid. Commissioned implementation needs a separate signed agreement before work begins. There is no automatic promise of employment, ownership, revenue share, academic credit, future compensation or a funded role. External benefits belong to the participant and are not studio compensation.
