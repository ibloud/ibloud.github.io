# AI-Assisted Development: Output, Coordination Debt, and Owner Control

*Loptr Lab · Preliminary case-study summary · October 26, 2026*

AI tools helped Loptr Lab produce prototypes, creator resources, automated tests, documentation, and accessibility repairs with comparatively low direct subscription expense. But fragmented navigation, repeated corrections, unclear source ownership, and release checks left incomplete also consumed substantial founder attention.

## Recorded progress

- Made-Sick: 76 automated tests, 35 local browser checks, and 18 live read-only checks reported at separate checkpoints.
- PIXIE reconciliation: 43 tests reported passing in a distinct checkpoint.
- Duet: offline browser recovery and rule-characterization test work; permanent engine and accessibility acceptance remain separate, unfinished milestones.

These checkpoint counts are **not additive proof of complete production acceptance**. Authentication, physical-device, screen-reader and other acceptance items were still pending in parts of the ecosystem.

## What went wrong

The key failure was not lack of generated code. It was coordination: overlapping site entrances and guides, inconsistent publication status, unapproved or insufficiently verified hosting dependencies, repetitive troubleshooting, and too much founder supervision to get coherent outcomes. Source access to an existing ChatGPT Sites implementation remained unresolved from Codex, despite preserved local work.

That does not establish that every problem was caused by AI. Some constraints arose from external authentication, hosting and testing environments. The preventable process failure was not stopping when an authorization issue was known, or not requiring owner approval before introducing a new dependency.

## Costs and comparison limits

A monthly LLM subscription and a salaried human team do not buy equivalent services. Lower access cost can make experimentation practical, but the true cost also includes founder review, integration, corrections, deployment validation, service fees, and rework. Any hourly-value examples are **illustrations, not audited expenses or measured savings**. A human-led alternative is hypothetical; a disciplined team might have avoided some coordination mistakes but would still incur cost and risk.

## Corrective operating model

1. Use the existing GitHub-backed source and verify each project's actual deployment authority.
2. Inspect existing work before creating any site, repository, guide, integration, or service.
3. Keep one bounded objective and require explicit owner approval for scope and platform changes.
4. Verify real user journeys, accessibility and source recoverability before claiming complete releases.
5. Stop repeating unauthenticated access attempts when the same blocker remains unresolved.
6. Preserve distinct project identities while consolidating navigation through existing maintained routes.

**Source and caveat:** Derived from Loptr Lab's October 2026 development checkpoints and ecosystem audit. The full retrospective prepared for Made-Sick remains an editorial draft until published. Exact total LLM expenditure, start-to-finish logged hours, founder labor valuation and the counterfactual productivity of a human team have not been independently established.
