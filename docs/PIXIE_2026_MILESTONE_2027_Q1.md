## What we are recognizing

Loptr Lab's build-in-public milestone is a transition from separately demonstrated creative prototypes toward a **repeatable, accessible creative-to-action method**: a person can describe something they want to make, discover credible resources, learn and test safely, identify community and public support, understand the costs and consequences, and complete a first documented action. This is a *direction and proposed pilot*, not evidence that the full journey is deployed or independently accepted.

The worked example is a teenager with limited resources who wants to repurpose an older electronic musical instrument. It demonstrates hardware feasibility checks, age-appropriate learning and supervision, local resource discovery, relevant civic education, realistic eligibility checks for funding, and a safe next step. The method should generalize to other creative ambitions **only after the example has been tested**.

## The method: evidence → options → consequences → next action → review

Every prototype action plan should include:

1. **Goal:** user-stated desired outcome, with no assumption of money, credentials or contacts.
2. **Inventory:** verified equipment, skills, time, permissions and constraints; no unnecessary sensitive collection.
3. **Source-backed possibilities:** official manuals, repair guides, educational resources, community support and their limitations.
4. **Feasibility and safety:** model/revision, firmware versus retrofit distinction, backup/recovery, qualified review and youth-specific safeguards.
5. **Resource strategy:** no-cost first, supervised local options, actual operating costs, accessible alternatives, funding eligibility.
6. **Civic literacy:** factual, nonpartisan explanation of who controls local libraries, schools, public meetings and program budgets, with official current sources; never political persuasion.
7. **One achievable action:** short task, completion criterion and a manual/resumable public checkpoint where consented.
8. **Review:** observed outcome, participant-approved evidence, barriers, cost, maintenance implications and whether to continue.

A teen should be able to start with identifying a device and learning MIDI fundamentals, without opening mains-powered equipment or needing to apply for a grant.
