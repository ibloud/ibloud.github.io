From a Wall of Eyes to an Ethics Hearing

How I use AI to develop a story: a record of my source material, associations and direction, alongside the assistant’s interpretation and generated draft.

Read PIXIE · Incident Review ↗Read the Markdown process record

The creative path

  1. Choose the image: a man facing a wall of watching eyes.
  2. Connect it to the graduate student trapped with PIXIE.
  3. Supply my earlier Fogg workshop from A to Z of Magic.
  4. Identify Synchronicity II and No Place Like Home as musical associations.
  5. Move into the rescuer’s viewpoint and introduce Dr. Meyers.
  6. Use AI to compare the material and draft a committee scene; record its additions.

1. The starting object

Dominique supplied renmakesmusic-story-4010159851270897080.mp4, a roughly 20-second clip. Its imagery shows a seated figure, viewed from behind, facing a wall of older televisions displaying eyes.

The assistant extracted and inspected still frames. It interpreted the image through surveillance, public scrutiny, identity, and the reversal of the viewer’s gaze. These were interpretations, not verified statements of Ren’s intent.

Capability limit: The assistant did not reliably listen to the audio. Its initial promise to listen overstated its available capability; it corrected that claim. Subsequent musical interpretation was grounded in the song references and lyrics Dominique supplied, not an analysis of the uploaded soundtrack. The clip’s music was not identified.

2. Dominique supplied the narrative situation

Dominique connected the image to a fictional graduate student injured after a laboratory power surge while connected to a Matrix/Animus-like environment. She becomes trapped; PIXIE fuses with her organic tissue; others describe her as “brain damaged.” Dominique proposed a university president’s voice or a research ethics committee meeting as the setting.

These elements came from Dominique. They were not discovered in the image by AI. Matrix and Animus were Dominique’s analogies, not verified technical descriptions or claims of affiliation.

Dominique required the assistant to ask when it could not access the referenced associations. The assistant initially asked for blog names before adequately checking the archive. Dominique corrected it and directed it to the archive.

3. What the archive did—and did not—establish

Retrieved earlier discussion distinguished Urban Alien Adventures / IBLoud IVXX, Loptr Lab, and PIXIE. An archived portfolio source identified https://pixie.pckt.blog/ as a reflective archive about systems, incentives, architecture, and creative practice. Other archived material identified The Rift Report as an existing publication.

The assistant also read the saved retrospective I Was Designing a Narrative Game Before I Knew How to Explain It. That AI-assisted retrospective describes Dominique’s earlier IBLoud Wattpad work inspired by The Magicians and Battle the Beast: music as navigation, persistent memory, altered timelines, and ethical choices. It is a secondary account, not a replacement for the original stories.

The specific A to Z of Magic passage and graduate-student incident were not recovered in that search. The assistant could not verify the proposed scene’s placement against complete fictional continuity. Public retrieval of the PIXIE blog also failed; that does not establish that the blog itself is unavailable.

4. Dominique supplied the primary story reference

Dominique pasted “Straight Outta Filory,” from A to Z of Magic by IBLoud. The supplied editor text marked it published and displayed 912 words. Those metadata were supplied by Dominique, not independently verified.

Cover reference: https://img.wattpad.com/cover/139868907-64-k546720.jpg — a cover image, not a full story permalink.

In the supplied passage, Dean Fogg addresses an audience in a workshop framed by lawyers and a software warning. He describes separating Aster from Luna to demonstrate his competence. Luna’s music helps balance Aster’s powers. The examination becomes catastrophic, Aster’s body is left comatose, and Fogg admits manipulating memories while trying to repair the consequences.

The narrative device—an authority figure revealing responsibility through an apparently instructional address—already existed in Dominique’s writing before this session. AI did not invent that device for her project.

Dominique explicitly identified The Police’s “Synchronicity II” and Todrick Hall’s “No Place Like Home” as the important musical references. The assistant proposed connections involving interdependence, return, identity, and who defines recovery. These are creative readings, not confirmed artist intent or proof that either song plays in the uploaded clip.

5. Dominique changed the viewpoint

Dominique then made the decisive connection: the man in the chair is trying to get her out, and his feelings cloud his judgement. She introduced Inpatient’s “Dr. Meyers” and pasted its lyrics.

That supplied text presents a doctor who distances himself from the children in his care, rationalizes harm through scientific and commercial objectives, and later seeks a breakthrough after Jeremiah’s death. The assistant worked from the pasted text; it did not independently verify the recording, credits, linked annotations, or broader Inpatient continuity.

Dominique supplied the association between this doctor and the would-be rescuer. The assistant articulated a possible dramatic parallel: scientific ambition and personal attachment can each become reasons to disregard the person supposedly being helped. This is an ethical analogy within the proposed fiction, not an assertion that the characters have identical motives or culpability.

6. What AI contributed to the scene

The assistant proposed a fictional incident-review transcript titled PIXIE · Incident Review, spoken by the man in the chair before a committee. It generated the scene’s wording, pacing, questions, stage directions, and ending.

New details invented by the assistant include: the student continuing to communicate; her warning that separation might hurt; the rescuer dismissing PIXIE as interference; an instruction to suspend extraction; her asking him to wait; and his attempting another extraction anyway. These were narrative proposals, not facts supplied by Dominique or recovered from her archive. They require her continuity review.

The draft also gives the man knowledge of Meyers’s account. Whether that connection is literal, metaphorical, musical, or part of the fictional world remains unresolved. No particular relationship between the man and the student was established.

The draft’s movement is from “I promised to get her out” towards asking what she needs. Its underlying questions are whether observation amounts to understanding, whether the rescuer can hear an answer he dislikes, and who has authority to define recovery.

8. What this example demonstrates

Dominique uses association across images, music, earlier fiction, and current projects to direct creative work. Here, AI helped put those materials beside one another, articulate relationships, and produce a reviewable scene. The direction developed through her references and corrections; it did not originate from an autonomous AI reading of the clip.

The example also exposes limitations worth retaining in a public account: incomplete retrieval, an overstated audio capability, and plausible scene details that could be mistaken for established canon if their origin were not recorded.
