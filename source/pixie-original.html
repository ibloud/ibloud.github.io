Make your own deck

Your story can become a deck.

MakePlayingCards is one suggested print service. You can develop your own playing deck or reflection deck independently; participation in PIXIE is not required. Our PIXIE concepts are not a downloadable licensed template.

  1. Choose the tool and countFor standard games, preserve readable ranks, suits, pips and the jokers your rules need. For an oracle, define your own entries and guide. Start with paper mockups.
  2. Prepare artwork you may useCreate original images or obtain written commercial permissions. Check fonts, symbols and packaging as well as illustrations. AI-provider terms and copyright protection are separate questions.
  3. Choose the printer product and templateVisit MakePlayingCards. Select size, card count, stock, finish and packaging, then use that product’s current template. Its image preparation guide lists bridge cards as 2.25 × 3.5 inches with a minimum 747 × 1122 pixel upload at 300 dpi. Confirm the selected template before exporting.
  4. Upload and review every cardKeep critical content inside safe areas and extend backgrounds through bleed. Check front order, back orientation, symmetry and the online preview. Do not rely on screen images to judge physical quality.
  5. Order a proof before a runInspect actual cuts, contrast, finish and packaging. Fan 13 cards and play full hands. Revise before ordering quantities; calculate shipping and replacements as well as printing.
  6. Choose your sales routeDirect sales and the MPC marketplace have different obligations. Confirm the printer’s current rights requirements, listing terms, prices and fulfillment arrangements before promising products.

Suggested workflow, not an affiliation or manufacturer approval. PIXIE’s two decks still need 108 finished fronts, a verified back, final guide and physical proof.

Credit & legacy

Say her name.
Keep the credit visible.

Pamela Colman Smith illustrated the Rider–Waite–Smith tarot deck, first published in 1909. Her visual contribution belongs in the story of those cards, not in a footnote. Read the V&A’s account.

Loptr Lab’s PIXIE is an original nonhuman character. This project’s attention to credit and creative economics is a response to Smith’s legacy; it is not an authorized continuation of her work, an estate project or a claim that she endorsed AI.
