# Loptr Lab mission and participation

**Owner-approved public position · October 1, 2026**

Loptr Lab is a pre-seed, people-over-profit, accessibility-first venture working toward a self-sustaining model within a capitalist economy. Money sustains the work; meaningful change for people is its purpose. We accept funding only on terms that keep people and accessibility first. Our long-term vision includes universal basic income. We aim to bring change to life and leave a transparent record of what we tried, what worked, and what failed so others can carry it forward. This mission governs our projects, funding decisions, and partnerships; it is not a temporary marketing position.

## Funding sustains the mission

Financial support is optional and sustains infrastructure, maintenance, accessibility work, and documented development. Paying does not buy contributor status, canon authority, approvals, ownership, or employment. Participation and accessibility are not sponsorship rewards. Project-specific licenses and existing signed agreements continue to apply.

Funding or partnership terms must preserve the mission. No funding amount, sponsorship tier, or roadmap changes this boundary. “Pre-seed” describes the venture's development stage here; it does not establish a completed investment round or particular legal-entity status.

Universal basic income is a long-term social vision, not a current payment program or a promised outcome of this venture. Our present work is to build accessible systems, explore sustainable support for creative and technical work, and publish an honest learning record.

## Participation and reviewer terms

Current open review and contribution opportunities are voluntary and unpaid. Before work begins, agree in writing on scope, time, what will be public, credit preferences, and an exit path. You can stop at any point. Participation does not promise employment, ownership, revenue share, academic credit, or future pay. Any paid commission or other formal arrangement requires a separate signed agreement before work begins. External assistance or benefits belong to the participant and are not compensation from Loptr Lab.

We welcome people who share the mission and want to contribute, learn, or advance their own work. Credit is optional and agreed with the participant. Recording and public identification require consent. Seeking paid work is legitimate; these currently unpaid opportunities should never be presented as paid roles or guaranteed career advancement.

## Accessibility and evidence

Accessibility is a design and operating priority, not a completed universal certification. State what is implemented, what was actually tested, and what remains pending. External human review, device checks, rights clearance, and release gates retain their own requirements; neither funding nor this mission statement substitutes for them.

### Accessibility creates opportunities

Technology should adapt to people. Disabled participants should be able to design, test, train, operate and benefit economically from it, rather than only access a finished product. Profit can sustain innovation; exclusion must not be its business model.

Apply this in existing project reviews: ask about functional access needs at the start; offer text-first, asynchronous and alternative-input paths; agree reasonable adaptations and revisit barriers. State whether a task is training, voluntary contribution or paid professional work before it starts. Commissioned production and research require agreed fair compensation and written terms; do not relabel them as obligatory unpaid playtesting.

Participants choose attribution and public visibility. Do not require diagnoses or biometric movement recordings to participate. Any necessary sensitive-data collection needs a specific purpose, minimisation, consent and a private route. Movement data must not be reused for AI training or another secondary purpose without the required rights and explicit authorisation. Record the task, adaptation, observed result and unresolved barrier—not a profile of the person.
